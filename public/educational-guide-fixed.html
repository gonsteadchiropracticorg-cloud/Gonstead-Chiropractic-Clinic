History & Evolution

Origins and Contributions by Dr. Clarence S. Gonstead

Clarence Selmer Gonstead (1898-1978) was a pivotal figure in chiropractic, transforming it from a rudimentary practice into a more scientifically grounded biomechanical discipline. Born on July 24, 1898, in Willow Lake, South Dakota, Gonstead's early life on a dairy farm in Primrose, Wisconsin, fostered a keen interest in mechanical problem-solving.

Gonstead's personal experience with severe rheumatoid arthritis, which was successfully treated by chiropractor Dr. J. B. Olson, proved to be a life-altering event. This experience inspired him to pursue chiropractic education. He graduated from the Palmer School of Chiropractic and Infirmary in 1923.

Key Innovation: The Gonstead Disc Concept

Gonstead, along with his younger brother Merton, developed the "Gonstead Disc Concept," which posited that the vertebral disc, rather than solely the vertebral bone, was often the primary cause of nerve pressure in subluxations. This concept was a significant departure from the prevailing theories and contributed to a more comprehensive understanding of discogenic back pain.

Core Principles & Diagnostic Tools

The Gonstead Chiropractic Technique is characterized by a meticulous and comprehensive approach to spinal analysis, relying on a five-component diagnostic framework to identify the precise location and nature of vertebral subluxations.

Five-Component Diagnostic Framework

  • Full-Spine Weight-Bearing X-rays: Utilizes 14x36 inch films for AP and lateral views to assess the entire spine
  • Static & Motion Palpation: Involves feeling the spine at rest and during movement to identify issues
  • Nervoscope/Thermography: Uses an instrument to detect heat differentials along the spine
  • Visual Assessment: Observes the patient's posture, gait, and body symmetry
  • Symptom/Case Management: Integrates patient symptoms with objective findings
